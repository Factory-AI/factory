Service accounts are non-human identities that automate work on behalf of your organization. Use them when a workload should belong to your organization instead of a specific teammate, such as CI/CD pipelines, scripts, Slack automations, or Droid Computers.

<Info>
  **Enterprise Feature** — Service accounts are available to enterprise customers. [Contact us](mailto:sales@factory.ai) to learn more.
</Info>

Every [agent profile](/enterprise/agent-profiles) also has its own service account, which is created with the profile and managed from the profile editor. This page covers service accounts you create yourself.

## How service accounts work

A service account authenticates with a Factory API key and runs as its own principal. Sessions, computers, billing events, and audit surfaces are attributed to the service account instead of a human user.

Service accounts must be active to authenticate. If you mark one inactive or delete it, new requests using its keys fail, and workloads depending on that identity should be moved or restarted with another account.

### Who can manage a service account

Managers and Owners can see and manage every service account in the **Service Accounts** section of the Identity page. A User who is an [owner](#owners) of a service account also sees the Service Accounts section and can manage the accounts they own.

| Action                                                        | Who can do it                                                              |
| ------------------------------------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Create a service account                                      | Managers and Owners                                                        |
| Edit, change status, or delete                                | Managers, Owners, and the account's owners                                 |
| Manage API keys, Git credentials, connectors, secrets, and computers | Managers, Owners, and the account's owners                          |
| Change owners and access                                      | Managers, Owners, and the account's owners                                 |
| Lock or unlock                                                | The account's owners and organization Owners                               |

While an account is [locked](#lock), only its owners and organization Owners can make any of these changes.

You can also manage owners, lock, and access through the Factory API. See the **API Reference** tab.

## Owners

Owners are people who manage a specific service account without needing the Manager role. Owners can view and edit the account, including its API keys, Git credentials, connectors, secrets, computers, access, and lock. They can also change its owners and delete it.

- Owners must be active members of the organization. Directory groups cannot be owners.
- An account can have up to 50 owners.
- Creating a service account does not make you one of its owners. Add owners from the account's settings.
- Owners who are not Managers cannot create new service accounts.

The service account of an agent profile has no owner list of its own. The profile's owners manage it. See [Agent Profiles](/enterprise/agent-profiles#owners).

---

## Lock

Lock a service account to stop other Managers from changing it. While an account is locked, only its owners and organization Owners can change its settings, status, owners, or access, manage its API keys, Git credentials, connectors, secrets, or computers, or delete it. Other Managers see it as read-only.

Only the account's owners and organization Owners can lock or unlock it. You can lock an account that has no owners after confirming; until someone adds an owner, only organization Owners can change it.

The service account of an agent profile follows the profile's lock.

---

## Access

Access controls who can use a service account: who can choose it in the **Run as** picker for an [automation](/web/automations), who can act as it from the session composer, and who can use its computers.

- **Everyone**: every member of the organization can use the account.
- **Specific people and groups**: only the people you list and members of the directory groups you select can use it. Managers and the account's owners can always use it.

People without access do not see the account in **Run as** pickers. Directory groups come from [Directory Sync (SCIM)](/enterprise/identity-sso-and-scim#directory-sync-scim-provisioning).

The access of an agent profile's service account is set in the profile editor. Org Default's account is always open to everyone.

## Computer secrets

Computer secrets are environment variables for a service account's managed computers. They are available on Factory-hosted deployments. Open them from **Secrets** in an active service account's actions, or from the **Secrets** tab of an [agent profile](/enterprise/agent-profiles).

- Service account secrets are added to your organization's computer secrets. If a name matches an organization secret, the service account's value is used.
- Existing computers keep their current environment until they are refreshed and restarted.
- Values are write-only. You cannot read a value back after saving it.
- Names can be up to 128 characters, must start with an uppercase letter or underscore, and can contain only uppercase letters, digits, and underscores. Some names are reserved and cannot be used.
- Values must be 1 to 10,000 characters.

Managers, Owners, and the account's owners can manage secrets. The account's lock applies. Inactive accounts cannot have their secrets changed.

## Manage access safely

<AccordionGroup>
  <Accordion title="Use short-lived keys when possible">
    Set expiration dates for keys that are used in temporary automation. Short-lived keys limit the blast radius if a key is accidentally exposed. Organizations can also set a maximum lifetime for new keys, including service account keys. See [API key lifetime limit](/enterprise/hierarchical-settings-and-org-control#api-key-lifetime-limit).
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="Rotate keys regularly">
    Create a new key, update the workload, then revoke the old key. Regular rotation reduces the risk of compromised credentials.
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="Keep names stable">
    Service account names are immutable identifiers. Create a new service account if you need a different name.
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="Lock shared accounts">
    Add owners and lock accounts that production workloads depend on, so only the people responsible for them can change them.
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="Delete when finished">
    Deleting a service account revokes its access and cleans up owned Factory resources where possible.
  </Accordion>
</AccordionGroup>
